## Policies

### Technology & AI Use Policy

#### In class

Laptops and tablets may be used for course-related purposes during lecture and lab sessions, unless the instructor explicitly states otherwise for a given activity. Phones should be put away during class — please keep them off your desk and on silent — unless needed for a specific course-related purpose.

#### Quizzes and lab quizzes

These are closed-book and closed-technology. No phones, laptops, tablets, or other electronic devices are allowed.

#### Exams

Exams are closed-book and closed-technology, with one exception: you may bring one page of handwritten notes. Typed or printed materials — including printouts of the slides — are not permitted and will result in a grade of zero on the exam. Because only handwritten notes are allowed, you're encouraged to take notes by hand throughout the semester to prepare.

### Use of AI/LLM Tools

By default, GW policy prohibits submitting AI-generated content for evaluation. Per the Office of the Provost's Generative AI Guidelines, students may not submit for evaluation any content — including code — that was generated, in whole or in part, by generative AI tools, unless the instructor explicitly grants permission otherwise. In this course, the following expectations apply, but the default prohibition governs everything not explicitly permitted below.

AI tools are not allowed during lab sessions. This includes ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, and any other generative AI tool, for any purpose, including debugging, code generation, or explanation. Labs are your opportunity to build and demonstrate your own problem-solving skills, and lab instructors may randomly ask you to explain your code — from the current lab or a prior assignment — on the spot. If you cannot explain code you submitted, this will be treated as evidence it was not your own work. TAs and the instructor may ask you at any time during lab to close unrelated applications or explain your current approach.

For homework assignments, the same expectation of explainability applies: you are expected to be able to explain any code or solution you submit.

Disclosure is mandatory for any AI use outside of lab. If you use an AI tool for any part of a homework assignment, you must explicitly disclose which parts and how it was used — for example, with a short note at the top of your submission (e.g., "AI-assisted: used ChatGPT to debug a segfault on line 42."). Undisclosed use that is later confirmed to be AI-generated will result in a zero for the entire assignment, not just the affected portion, and may additionally be reported as a violation of the GW Code of Academic Integrity (see below), since submitting undisclosed AI-generated work as your own is independently classified as cheating and plagiarism under university policy.

**Appropriate use (homework only):** AI tools can be a helpful tutor — for example, asking them to explain a concept you're stuck on, clarify a definition, or walk through why an algorithm works. They should not be used to write code on your behalf. Struggling with the implementation is where the learning happens, and outsourcing it defeats the purpose of the assignment.

This policy is a course-specific application of the GW Code of Academic Integrity and the university's Generative AI Guidelines. Violations of the policies above — including AI use during lab, undisclosed AI use on homework, submission of fully AI-generated work, and unauthorized technology during quizzes/exams — will be treated as academic integrity violations and may be referred to Conflict Education & Student Accountability (CESA), in addition to any grade penalty described here.
